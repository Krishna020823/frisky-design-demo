Buzz & Bond

A name people remember and a platform that runs the whole community: what’s on, booking and payment, tickets on WhatsApp, and a console the team uses to run every event.

Buzz & Bond, Ranchi

Logo, UX/UI design, development, admin console

Custom-coded on Laravel
Live
buzzandbond.com

The Brief

Buzz & Bond brings strangers together in Ranchi over books and music: Sunday book clubs in cafés, unplugged music evenings, game nights and meetups, with its own community space, the Culture Floor, on the way.

The brief had two halves. The community needed an identity as warm and social as its events. And it needed one place where people could see what’s on, book a seat and pay in a minute, plus a back office where a small team could run events, payments, refunds and the door without calling a developer.

The animated wordmark from the Buzz & Bond footer, running live. The letters rise in the first time it scrolls into view, then the Zs buzz and the ampersand beats. Hover over a letter.

The Logo

The wordmark is set in heavy geometric capitals with tight spacing, so BUZZ and BOND lock together around an ampersand cut at the same weight. The bond is built into the name itself. The twin Zs sit almost touching, which gives the first word its hum.

The mark is drawn as a single vector, so it stays sharp from a phone header to a large banner, and works in orange, white or espresso brown. For small spaces, the two Bs and the ampersand become a B&B monogram on a rounded orange tile, used as the browser icon and the phone home-screen icon.

Colour and Type

Buzz Orange leads everything, from buttons to the footer. Espresso brown replaces black, so even the dark surfaces stay warm, with Ember, Marigold and Blush as supporting tones. Plus Jakarta Sans carries the interface: friendly, round and very legible on a phone. The same tokens run through the public site and the admin console, so they feel like one product.

Discover: Everything on One Scroll

The homepage answers two questions: what’s on, and when am I free? A featured-events carousel leads into a row of upcoming Sundays you can tap, then memberships, standout past events with video, photo highlights from recent meetups, and reviews from the community. Every section is managed from the admin console.

Designed for the Phone First

Events are found on Instagram and shared in WhatsApp groups, and those links open on a phone, so the mobile layout came first. It works like an app: a bottom navigation bar, search across events, venues and hosts, filters for today, tomorrow and the weekend, and a calendar that marks every day with something on.

Book: Four Steps to a Seat

  1. 01Pick a showOne listing can run many dates and show times, each with its own seat count.
  2. 02Add ticketsTicket types, coupons, and fees and GST shown up front.
  3. 03Log in by phoneNew guests verify their mobile number once with a WhatsApp code.
  4. 04PayUPI, cards and wallets through Razorpay.

A weekly book club is one page with eleven bookable Sundays, not eleven separate pages. When a show fills up or nears its start time, sales stop by themselves. The ticket arrives on WhatsApp with a QR code, and the same channel carries reminders, event updates and refund notices. Monthly and single-day memberships are sold the same way.

The Admin Console

Behind the site is a console built around how the team actually works. Its sections follow the sidebar’s six groups:

Overview

  • Live dashboard
  • QR check-in

Events

  • Events and shows
  • Categories
  • Venues
  • Cities

Homepage

  • Featured events
  • Banners
  • Event showcase
  • Past highlights
  • Reviews
  • Text and pages

Membership

  • Members
  • Plans and prices

Sales

  • Bookings
  • Customers
  • Payments
  • Refunds
  • Coupons and offers

System

  • WhatsApp log
  • Refund policy
  • Settings
  • Admin users and roles
  • Audit log
  • Backups

The dashboard shows ticket and membership revenue, bookings, refunds and seats held in checkout right now, with trends by day, category and payment method.

Figures blurred for client privacy.

Events are written and published in plain language: title, venue, host, descriptions, images, ticket types and every date and show time with its own stock. Images are converted to modern formats on upload, so the team never has to resize a photo. One-click actions duplicate an event, unpublish it, cancel it and refund everyone, or send a WhatsApp update to everyone booked.

The homepage is edited from the console too, from the order of the hero carousel to the featured stories, highlights and reviews. On the night, any team member’s phone becomes the door scanner: it reads the QR on a ticket, checks the guest in and turns away tickets for a different event.

Refunds follow tiers the team sets, such as a full refund up to 72 hours before a show and half up to 24 hours, and guests can cancel online from their tickets. Bookings export to CSV or Excel. Ready-made roles give Check-in Staff, Finance and Managers only the access they need, and every admin action is recorded in the audit log.

Under the Hood

  • Custom-coded on Laravel and PHP 8.3, with no page builder or theme
  • Razorpay payments, with refunds sent automatically or after approval
  • WhatsApp messaging for login codes, tickets, reminders and updates
  • Role-based admin access, sign-in lockout after repeated failed attempts, and a full audit log
  • Nightly database backups, kept for 14 days
  • HTTPS with HSTS and security headers, clean URLs, an XML sitemap and share previews for every event

Outcome

Buzz & Bond now runs its calendar from one place. The team publishes an event in minutes, guests book and pay on their phones, tickets arrive on WhatsApp, and the door is checked from the same system that sold the seats. The brand ties all of it together, from the site header to the ticket in a guest’s chat. Read more about what makes a site like this convert in What Makes a Brand Website Convert.
